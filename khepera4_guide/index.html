Module 2 Differential-Drive Kinematics (How 2 Wheels Steer)

Connecting wheel rotations $\omega_L, \omega_R$ directly to chassis speeds $v, \omega$.

1 Forward Kinematics: Wheels $\longrightarrow$ Chassis

If the motors are spinning at angular velocities $\omega_R$ and $\omega_L$ (in radians/second), what is the resulting forward speed $v$ and turn rate $\omega$ of the robot?

$$v = \frac{r}{2} (\omega_R + \omega_L) \quad \text{[Forward Linear Velocity, m/s]}$$ $$\omega = \frac{r}{L} (\omega_R - \omega_L) \quad \text{[Angular Turn Rate, rad/s]}$$

Intuition: Forward speed $v$ is simply the average speed of the two wheels. Turning rate $\omega$ is proportional to the difference between the right and left wheel speeds.

2 Inverse Kinematics: Chassis $\longrightarrow$ Wheels

When our ROS 2 controller outputs a command on /cmd_vel specifying linear speed $v$ and turn rate $\omega$, how does the robot know how fast each wheel must spin?

$$\omega_R = \frac{v + \frac{L}{2} \omega}{r} \quad \text{[Right Wheel Speed Setpoint]}$$ $$\omega_L = \frac{v - \frac{L}{2} \omega}{r} \quad \text{[Left Wheel Speed Setpoint]}$$

In Gazebo, the DiffDrive plugin solves these exact inverse kinematic equations 1000 times per second to command the virtual wheel joints.

Module 3 Inside the Robot's Brain: The 3-Step Closed Loop

Why unicycles cannot move sideways, and how Lyapunov polar coordinates steer the robot accurately onto target waypoints.

1

Perception

Every 20 ms ($50\text{ Hz}$), optical encoders count wheel ticks. Dead reckoning calculates the robot's real pose:

$$\begin{bmatrix} x(t+\Delta t) \\ y(t+\Delta t) \\ \theta(t+\Delta t) \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} x + v\cos(\theta)\Delta t \\ y + v\sin(\theta)\Delta t \\ \theta + \omega\Delta t \end{bmatrix}$$

Published on topic /odom as nav_msgs/Odometry.

2

Error Dynamics

The target is at $(x^*, y^*)$. A unicycle cannot drive sideways, so we transform Cartesian error into polar coordinates:

$$\rho = \sqrt{\Delta x^2 + \Delta y^2} \quad \text{[Distance to goal]}$$ $$\alpha = \text{atan2}(\Delta y, \Delta x) - \theta \quad \text{[Heading steering error]}$$ $$\beta = -\theta - \alpha + \theta^* \quad \text{[Goal orientation error]}$$

$\alpha$ tells the robot how much to steer to point at the goal. $\rho$ tells it how far away it is.

3

Cascade Control

Lyapunov stability criteria teaches us how to choose velocity setpoints that guarantee smooth convergence without spinning out:

$$v^* = \text{clamp}(K_\rho \cdot \rho \cdot \cos\alpha, -v_{\max}, v_{\max})$$ $$\omega^* = \text{clamp}(K_\alpha \cdot \alpha + K_\beta \cdot \beta, -\omega_{\max}, \omega_{\max})$$

Published on /cmd_vel. As the robot gets closer ($\rho \to 0$), speed $v^*$ smoothly decelerates to zero!

The 4 Key Engineering Indicators

  • 1. Maximum Overshoot ($\sigma\%$, *Suprareglarea*):

    How much the robot exceeds the target before stopping. Formula: $\sigma = e^{-\frac{\pi\zeta}{\sqrt{1-\zeta^2}}} \times 100\%$.

  • 2. Settling Time ($t_s$, *Timpul de reglare*):

    The time after which error remains permanently inside the $2\%$ band. Approx: $t_s \approx \frac{4}{\zeta \omega_n}$.

  • 3. Rise Time ($t_r$, *Timpul de creștere*):

    Time to accelerate from 10% to 90% of setpoint. Approx: $t_r \approx \frac{\pi - \arccos(\zeta)}{\omega_n \sqrt{1-\zeta^2}}$.

  • 4. Steady-State Error ($e_{st}$, *Eroarea staționară*):

    Final offset after coming to a complete standstill ($e_{st} \le 2\text{ cm}$ in our system).

💡 Why Prof. Dumitrache Emphasizes $\zeta = \frac{\sqrt{2}}{2} \approx 0.707$:
It is the mathematically proven optimal balance: it achieves the fastest possible settling time with a minimal, safe overshoot of strictly $\le 4.3\%$.

Module 6 Workspace Architecture & Command Cheat Sheet

Exact terminal commands to run the real simulation and Qt dashboard.

Quick Terminal Commands

1. Launch Simulation + Dashboard together (1-Click): 
ros2 launch khepera4_control control_station.launch.py
2. Or launch Gazebo Simulation only: 
ros2 launch khepera4_description khepera4_sim.launch.py
3. In a 2nd terminal, launch Qt Control Dashboard: 
ros2 run khepera4_control control_dashboard
4. Or run automated step benchmark directly: 
ros2 run khepera4_control closed_loop_controller --ros-args -p mode:=step -p goal_x:=0.5
